1. Breakthrough Progress in Quantum Computing

China's independently developed quantum computer, "Origin Wukong," has for the first time in the world run a billion-parameter AI fine-tuning model. This marks the beginning of efforts in China to use a quantum-classical hybrid framework to transform large-model parameter optimization into a quantum annealing problem, enabling quantum computers to assist with AI large-model tasks.

This will help accelerate the practical deployment of domestically developed AI large models. At the same time, because of the computing advantages of quantum computers, once the relevant technical barriers are overcome, the development of domestic computing power will be less constrained by chip restrictions imposed by the United States and other Western countries. In addition, quantum computing can prioritize computationally intensive tasks such as high-dimensional parameter optimization and attention mechanisms, complementing classical computing.

Combined with China's existing AI chip industry, the two technologies could create a synergistic effect greater than the sum of their parts. However, quantum computers still face many challenges before they can be widely commercialized. For example, the number of qubits would need to increase from 72 to the thousand-qubit level, while quantum gate fidelity would need to improve from 99.5% to 99.99%, requiring breakthroughs in key technologies such as superconducting materials and error correction.

Quantum computing also needs to be deeply integrated with classical systems, while ecosystems such as compilers and development toolchains must mature to lower the barrier to adoption. At present, China has begun to take a leading position in certain areas of quantum computing. Combined with recent U.S. tariff measures, the overall research and development landscape is continuing to change.

2. AI-Assisted Diagnosis Accuracy Exceeds 95%

Recently, U.S. technology company DeepHealth announced that its AI-powered medical diagnostic system achieved an accuracy rate of more than 95% in breast cancer screening, exceeding the average level of human doctors. Using deep learning technology, the system can rapidly analyze medical images, identify early cancerous lesions, and provide diagnostic recommendations.

DeepHealth's AI system is currently being piloted in several hospitals in the United States and may be expanded globally in the future. Experts say that the introduction of AI technology could significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of medical diagnosis and lead to better treatment outcomes for patients.

"This is a milestone in the development of medical AI" — Quoted from WSWW, Editor-in-Chief of Technology Report
